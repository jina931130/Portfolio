Chapter 01

Background and problem

01 · Background

Drivers warn, thank and give way
with gestures and hazard lights

Drivers thank others with hazard lights or a wave, warn with hazard lights and the horn, and give way with indicators and hand signals. These are brief signals, yet they let strangers align their intentions and cooperate, so we treated them as the starting point for autonomous driving.

Conclusion

Driver signals create cooperation on the road, but communication between autonomous vehicles does not yet capture them.

02 · Limitation

Existing V2V centres on safety and misses
the communication of manual driving

Commercial V2V today exchanges information for safety and traffic flow, such as junction collision warnings, signal data and platooning. The SAE J2735 standard defines messages like position, speed and signal phase, but none for emotional messages such as thanks or giving way. So we saw a need to go beyond safety data and redesign the communication of manual driving for autonomous vehicles.

03 · Prior Research

Pedestrian signals are well studied;
vehicle-to-vehicle design is not

Between pedestrians and vehicles, studies on eHMI and pixel lights have shown how visual signals convey intent and emotion. Research also found that vehicle-to-vehicle communication raises passengers' sense of security and trust, but design research on how passengers see and handle that communication was lacking.

07 · Classification

We split the five intents into
emotional and functional

The five intents were split by their importance to driving and their nature. Thanks, giving way and apology are not essential to driving but carry emotional communication between drivers; caution and stopping are safety information passengers must receive. This split later decided where and how each intent appears in the interface.

09 · Display

We chose the 17-inch centre display
for familiar, phone-like touch input

Because passengers choose and send intents themselves while driving, the screen had to be easy to reach and familiar to use. The centre display works by touch like a phone, and its split view of navigation and vehicle status left room for a communication area.

11 · Color Coding

Each intent has its own colour;
thanks and apology differ in brightness

Each intent has its own colour so it can be recognised without looking long at the screen. The colours follow road sign and warning light conventions, and thanks and apology, both blue, differ in brightness by the weight of the message.

14 · Task Result

Only when sending caution did
five of six miss the button

All six sent thanks, giving way and apology without trouble, and one stalled on stop. Only with caution did five fail to spot the button straight away, owing to an unfamiliar interface and a small button. Functional intents that must be pressed in a hurry need larger, clearer buttons.

Chapter 05

Principles and next steps

16 · Design Principles

Vehicle-to-vehicle messages must be
quick to send and clear to receive

We separated points raised by many participants from suggestions made by a few, and turned them into principles for the second interface. The shared points were all about whether sending and receiving felt certain.

Shared feedback

Points raised by many participants

01

SEND

Buttons easy to press

Larger buttons that can be found and pressed even in a hurry.

“You have to press it fast in an emergency, so the buttons could be bigger.”

02

RECEIVE

Clear send and receive feedback

Signal received intents with sound or a flash, and show when a sent intent has gone.

“Hazard lights feel definite when you switch them on and off; this signal feels unclear.”

03

FUNCTIONAL

Make functional intents clearer

Caution and stop must be as instantly clear as hazard lights and reach passengers unmistakably.

“For caution and stopping, I'm used to hazard lights.”

Further suggestions

Ideas from individual participants

Information at eye level

Show received intents where the eyes rest, not at the bottom.

P2

Unambiguous expression

The give-way and apology icons overlapped, so test emoji and text combinations separately.

P3 · P4

Suggestions and auto-send

Suggest or auto-send the right intent to ease input.

P3 · P5

Cultural differences

Text and colour read differently across cultures, so review them by language.
